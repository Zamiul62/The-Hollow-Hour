*This story is about the death of a child. It is a true event with no crime involved, and it is told here with care.*

The video lasts only a few seconds, and it is hard to forget.

A man in a shirt stands in a brown river, microphone in hand, the water rising toward his chest. He is walking out to show viewers how deep the river is at the spot where a teenager was last seen. Then he stops. His body jerks. He backs away fast, glancing over his shoulder.

"I think there's something down here at the bottom of the water," he says to his team.

He retreats toward the shallows and looks again at the place he just left. "It looked like an arm," he says. "Could it be her? But it might be a fish, too. I don't know."

## The girl everyone was looking for

The river was the Mearim, in the city of Bacabal in the northeastern Brazilian state of Maranhão. The girl was Raíssa, 13 years old.

According to Brazilian news reports, Raíssa was swimming in the river with friends when she drowned, and she had been missing since the day before the video was filmed. Her disappearance drew firefighters, a news crew and a worried town to the riverbank.

A child was missing in a river that many people there use, and every hour counted.

## The reporter

Lenildo Frazão covers police and community news for a local outlet in the region. On June 30, he went into the river to film part of his segment. His plan was simple: show where the girl had been swimming and how deep the water was.

He picked a spot and walked in. What happened next was caught on camera.

## What happened after the video

In the minutes and hours after the exchange on camera, firefighters from the Maranhão Military Fire Brigade and divers resumed the search on the morning of June 30. Reports say they went back to the area the reporter had pointed out. Raíssa's body was recovered there.

Authorities took it to the Civil Police and the medical examiner's institute. Their conclusion was that she had drowned accidentally. There were no signs of violence.

Raíssa was buried that evening. Her school declared three days of official mourning, and friends and family held a vigil with prayers and candles.

## What is known and what is not

Many headlines said the reporter "stepped on" the girl's body. That is how the story spread, but it is worth being careful.

The video shows him reacting to something in the water. He says himself that it might have been a fish. Several news outlets wrote that he stepped on or touched the body, but others noted that it was unclear whether he really did. What is documented is that the body was found at the spot where he was filming. Whether he made contact with her is not confirmed, and he had not made an official statement at the time those reports were published.

This is also why the story is in this section and not under mysteries. The cause of death is not in doubt. What gives it its eerie quality is a coincidence: a camera, a man who walked into the river to demonstrate a depth, and a search that, it turned out, was standing right beside the answer.

## The internet's reaction

The clip spread quickly on social media. Reactions ranged from horror to sympathy to what one outlet described as relief that the family finally had an answer. Some people made jokes about the video, including remarks about being afraid to swim in rivers or the sea. Those comments say something uncomfortable about how a family's worst moment can be turned into content in a matter of hours.

There was a real girl at the centre of the video. For her family and her classmates, it was the end of a search, not a viral moment.

## A note on river safety

Drowning is often quick and silent. It does not always look like the splashing and shouting people expect, and it can happen close to friends and in water that looks calm. Rivers add currents, drop-offs and murky water that make it hard to see what is below. Child-safety organizations recommend constant adult supervision for children near open water and teaching them to swim.
